# Caldova - Tech Transfer Playbook 2026

## Moving Production to a Contract Manufacturer

This playbook gives planning, quality, and procurement teams the standard sequence for
transferring production to a contract manufacturing organization (CMO). The internal knowledge
assistant is grounded on this document.

A tech transfer is the work of moving a validated process from one site to another so the
receiving site can make the same product to the same standard. Nothing ships from a new site
until the transfer is complete and quality has released the batch.

---

## Transfer Stages

### Stage 1 - Feasibility
**Typical duration:** 2 weeks

Confirm the receiving site can actually make the product before committing spend.

**Activities:**
- Confirm the CMO holds current qualification for the tier of work
- Check the quality agreement is in place and in date
- Compare equipment trains and identify any process differences
- Produce a first cost estimate using the tier rate and priority multiplier

**Exit criteria:** Written feasibility summary and an approved cost estimate

---

### Stage 2 - Technical Package
**Typical duration:** 3 weeks

Assemble everything the receiving site needs to run the process.

**Activities:**
- Transfer the master batch record, specifications, and analytical methods
- Agree in-process controls and sampling points
- Confirm material sources and lead times for all components
- Raise the change control covering the transfer

**Exit criteria:** Approved change control and a complete technical package

---

### Stage 3 - Engineering Runs
**Typical duration:** 4 weeks

Prove the process works on the receiving site's equipment.

**Activities:**
- Run at least one engineering batch at full scale
- Compare results against the sending site's process capability
- Resolve any differences before validation begins

**Exit criteria:** Engineering batch meets specification

---

### Stage 4 - Validation
**Typical duration:** 6 weeks

Demonstrate the process is reproducible.

**Activities:**
- Run three consecutive validation batches
- Complete analytical method verification at the receiving site
- Start stability sampling

**Exit criteria:** Three successful validation batches and an approved validation report

---

### Stage 5 - Commercial Release
**Typical duration:** 2 weeks

**Activities:**
- Quality review of complete batch records
- Confirm all test results are in specification
- Quality signature and release

**Exit criteria:** Batch released for commercial supply

---

## Total Timelines

| Route | Stages | Typical total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Packaging only | 1, 2, 5 | 6 weeks |
| Oral solid dose | 1-5 | 17 weeks |
| Sterile | 1-5 plus sterility validation | 22 weeks |

Priority affects how quickly a CMO staffs the work, not how long validation takes. Fast track
and emergency priority shorten queue time only. Validation cannot be compressed.

---

## Common Failure Points

- **Artwork changes:** printed packaging adds 4 weeks whenever artwork changes
- **Method transfer:** analytical method verification is the most common cause of delay
- **Material lead times:** sterile components run to 6 weeks and must be ordered at Stage 2
- **Open audit findings:** a CMO with open findings cannot start Stage 3

---

## Roles

- **Planning:** owns the schedule and the capacity request
- **Procurement:** owns the commercial relationship and the cost estimate
- **Quality:** owns the change control, validation review, and batch release
- **Site technical:** owns the batch record and the engineering runs

An assistant may draft summaries, estimates, and requests at any stage. It must not change a
record in a production system or a supplier portal. Every change goes through change control
and is approved by a person.
